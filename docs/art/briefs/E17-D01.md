## Spark Ball — `ball.spark`

Rarity: Uncommon. Tags: `scatter`, `strike`, `physics`.

Authored trigger: On the ball's first direct contact with a standing pin in its roll, every other standing pin whose centre lies within 0.35 m of that pin receives one horizontal impulse of 0.9 N·s directed away from it. Once per roll. Pins it moves count for this roll through the existing impulse attribution, one credit per pin. Radius and impulse are provisional (E24).

Required presentation: While armed, show the 0.35 m burst radius on the pin deck. The burst reads as a ring that does not hide pins; reduced motion shows a static ring.

Construction: a dark enamel sphere with four raised copper conductor forks and a broken radial star inlay around the finger holes. Keep the forks broad enough for a 48px icon; avoid Echo Ball's concentric-ring surface. Card lighting catches ceramic and copper, rather than hiding the object inside a spark cloud.

Armed view: a thin, grounded 0.35m radius guide on the pin deck, using a dotted circumference and a radial notch. Activation is centered on the actual first directly contacted standing pin: one expanding, broken ring with outward spokes; pins and contacts remain visible. Do not imply a burst on every hit, a score multiplier or an electrical damage status. Audio: one dry ceramic snap with a brief copper resonance. Reduced: one static broken ring and the same center marker. Radius and impulse remain E24-tunable data, never baked into art.

## Ghost Ball — `ball.ghost`

Rarity: Rare. Tags: `precision`, `phase`, `backrow`.

Authored trigger: The first standing pin the ball touches in its roll is passed through: no contact force either way, and the ball does not knock it down (it may still fall from other pins). When that contact ends the ball is solid for the rest of the roll. Once per roll.

Required presentation: Readable as translucent until its first contact and solid after it, without relying on colour; the passed pin flickers once.

Construction: a pale smoked-glass sphere with broad interrupted shell bands, open crescent-shaped negative spaces and a strong opaque rim. A checker/stipple interior distinguishes the phase-ready state even in grayscale. Keep the physical ball silhouette and its three holes readable; this is not a second floating ball or a renamed Echo skin.

Armed/initial roll state is visibly translucent and broken-lined. At the first passed contact, the specific pin gets one restrained outline flicker and a pass-through marker; when that contact ends, the sphere becomes solid with continuous shell bands. The pin may later fall from other pins, so never show permanent immunity. Audio: a short airy glass glide followed by a muted solid latch. Reduced motion/flash: static dotted-to-solid state change and a one-time pin outline, no flicker. No cue for later ordinary contacts.

## Splitter Ball — `ball.splitter`

Rarity: Epic. Tags: `coverage`, `spare`, `physics`.

Authored trigger: When the ball crosses the arrows line (4.57 m past the foul line) it splits into two balls, each 0.55× the original mass, diverging ±4° from its heading and keeping its spin. Both halves are the same roll: each pin either knocks down is credited once to that roll. A ball already in a gutter before the line does not split. Split point, mass and angle are provisional (E24).

Required presentation: Show the split line (the arrows) while armed. The split is a clear two-way break; reduced motion keeps both halves without trails.

Construction: two interlocking lobed shell halves joined by a deep stepped central seam, with paired outward chevrons. Use a solid warm ivory/brass construction and visible join hardware, distinct from Ghost's perforated translucent shell. The card shows one assembled ball; a small diagram can explain the later fork without implying two cards or two rolls.

Armed view marks the arrows line at 4.57m. Only the actual split event produces two clearly separated half identities, diverging at the authored ±4°; both retain their own spin and belong to the same roll. Show a shared roll marker, and never duplicate pin credits or present a ×2 score. A pre-line gutter shows no split. Audio: a paired mechanical latch, not an explosion. Reduced: two solid halves with no trails. Split position, mass and divergence remain tunable mechanic data.

## Gold Ball — `ball.gold`

Rarity: Common. Tags: `economy`, `gold`.

Authored trigger: After the roll resolves, pay $1 for every three distinct pins knocked down in that roll by any cause, at most $3 per roll. Paid with the frame's income after banking; currency is never retriggered.

Required presentation: A coin tally of pins in threes during resolution; the $ amount appears beside the frame income.

Construction: a thin-walled spherical vault shell with broad polygonal coin panels, a slot-shaped inset and three grouped tally pips. This must not be a gold recolor of House Ball or Hammer: no heavy crossed reinforcement bands, and a visibly lighter hollow construction supports the 0.85× mass tradeoff. Finger holes and spherical category remain clear.

Resolution groups distinct fallen pins in threes, using three-pip brackets that fill a coin tally. Display the actual $ amount beside frame income only after banking: $1 per three unique pins, capped at $3 per roll. Two rolls remain separate tallies. No payout sound on every collision or on retriggers. Audio: one small coin click per earned tally step, bounded to the actual cap. Reduced: static grouped pips and final amount. Keep Gold Pin's individual payout and Gold Rush's Mult fan visually and verbally separate.

## Glass Pin — `pin.glass`

Rarity: Uncommon. Tags: `value`, `fragile`, `glass`.

Authored trigger: +30 Value when knocked down. After the frame banks, each Glass pin that fell in that frame has a 20% chance to become a Wood pin in the same rack position. The roll uses the attempt's effects stream, and the change is saved. The rack never loses a slot. The chance is provisional (E24).

Required presentation: Clearly glass in silhouette and marking (not only colour). The crack-to-Wood change is announced after banking and is visible in the rack diagram.

Construction: a fluted blown-glass pin with thick beveled edges, a hollow neck window and interrupted facet highlights. Use a clear structural silhouette and a small etched fracture glyph; color alone cannot carry its identity. Ruby keeps an opaque body with inset stones, while Glass is transparent construction throughout. Decorative geometry must not silently alter the collision profile.

A normal fall displays +30 Value. Only an authoritative post-bank break event cracks the identity and replaces it with the existing Wood look in the same rack slot; announce the material change in the flat rack diagram. The 20% chance is neither an always-break animation nor a chance rolled by presentation. The slot never disappears. Audio: clear glass tick on fall, a separate short brittle crack only on conversion. Reduced: a static crack badge followed by Wood, with no shard cloud. Saved material changes come from E18.

## Echo Pin — `pin.echo`

Rarity: Rare. Tags: `echo`, `rack_order`, `retrigger`.

Authored trigger: +10 Value when knocked down; then repeats the basic Value and Mult effects (add_value, add_mult) of the pin that fell immediately before it in this frame's resolution order. It never copies retriggers, payouts, growth or breakage, and copies nothing when it falls first. Up to four in a rack.

Required presentation: The score log and the flat rack diagram must show which pin it echoed; an arrow from the copied pin is enough.

Construction: a pin with a split tuning-fork collar, linked rectangular resonator plates and paired return chevrons. Keep the central pin body solid and readable. Avoid simply moving Echo Ball's circular rings onto a pin: the linked plates communicate copying a predecessor, whereas the ball repeats its first direct target.

During resolution, draw a thin arrow from the actual copied pin to this Echo Pin in the flat rack diagram, and name that source in the score log. The source is the immediately previous pin in resolution order, not necessarily the spatial neighbor. Echo Pin always has its own +10 when fallen; then only the predecessor's basic add_value/add_mult are repeated. Never illustrate copied money, growth, breakage or another retrigger. If it resolves first, show no incoming arrow. Audio: a quiet paired resonator tap; reduced uses one static source arrow and copied-operation labels.

## Crown Pin — `pin.crown`

Rarity: Rare. Tags: `precision`, `positional`, `crown`.

Authored trigger: +40 Value only if it is the last distinct pin to fall in the frame, by resolution order; otherwise it adds nothing, not even base Value. Up to two in a rack (only one can be last).

Required presentation: Crowned silhouette. During resolution, show whether it fell last (the +40) or did not (a clear 0).

Construction: an opaque dark pin wearing a pronounced three-point brass crown and a V-shaped ceremonial collar. It is the last fallen pin in resolution order, not the last standing pin; never celebrate merely being left upright. If two Crowns are equipped, only the actual last one gets the positive cue. Audio: one restrained brass flourish only on the +40 event; the zero state stays quiet and readable. Reduced keeps the crown/0 state without a flourish animation.

## Reserve Call — `consumable.reserve_call`

Rarity: Common. Tags: `tactical`, `rack`, `reserve`.

Authored trigger: Play at the start of a frame, before roll 1: swap one reserve pin into a chosen rack position for this frame only. The displaced pin waits in that reserve slot and both return to their places after the frame. Exhausted for the run.

Required presentation: The swap shows on the rack diagram and the lane, with a clear 'this frame only' marker.

Construction: two offset equipment trays, one holding a pin and one showing a rack socket, joined by opposing exchange arrows. A small frame-shaped tab communicates temporary use. Keep the two-tray outline visibly distinct from Nudge's single map tile; avoid a generic telephone icon that does not explain the swap.

Before roll 1, preview the actual reserve pin and chosen rack slot. The displaced pin appears in the corresponding reserve slot, and both views carry an explicit “this frame only” marker. Show the authoritative return after the frame; never imply permanent acquisition, an extra rack slot or a replacement whose material is lost. Audio: two ordered wooden tray clicks, with a quieter return cue. Reduced uses static paired arrows and persistent temporary labels. It exhausts after use. ## Rule Waiver — `consumable.rule_waiver`

Rarity: Uncommon. Tags: `tactical`, `counter`, `boss`.

Authored trigger: Play before a throw in a boss match: the rival's rule does not apply for the rest of the current frame. Dead Straight allows charged spin; Clean Sweep banks that frame as scored even if it is open. Exhausted for the run.

Required presentation: The rule badge visibly waived for the frame; the rival may react, but never blocks input.

Construction: a folded ivory permit with a bold diagonal exemption seal and a short frame-duration tab. Use a broad angular paper silhouette rather than Encore's paired tickets or Standing Order's clipboard. An empty document face avoids unreadable baked-in legal text; UI carries the actual rule name and duration.

Only a confirmed use before a throw in a boss match places a clear diagonal waived mark over the current rule badge, with “waived this frame” text. Dead Straight's spin availability and Clean Sweep's banking behavior are reported from E18, never toggled by art code. The exemption lasts through the rest of that frame and then visibly expires; it does not defeat the rival or permanently remove the rule. A rival reaction must use the existing cooldown and never block input. Audio: one dry seal stamp; reduced uses a static strike-through and expiry-state change. ## Distinct-type flags and E19 acceptance checks

The following pairs are **flagged for explicit side-by-side review**, not accepted
as distinct merely because their names or colors differ:

| Pair | Confusion risk | Required separating evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Spark Ball / Fuse Pin | Both produce a local outward ring | Sphere with conductor forks versus pin with a crooked wick; first direct contact/once per roll versus first Fuse fall/once per frame, and different ring notches/audio |
| Spark Ball / existing Echo Ball | A ring could imply the same retrigger | Radial conductor construction and physical contact-center ring versus nested resonator surface and score-log repeat cue |
| Echo Pin / existing Echo Ball | Shared name and echo tags | Linked rectangular collar plates and predecessor arrow versus sphere/rings and first-direct-pin retrigger; copied operations explicitly limited |
| Glass Pin / Ghost Ball | Both translucent | Fluted hollow pin and post-bank fracture-to-Wood versus interrupted spherical shell and first-contact phase-to-solid; clarity in grayscale |
| Glass Pin / existing Ruby Pin | Facets could reduce to a recolor | Entire transparent fluted construction with etched fracture mark versus opaque pin with inset jewels |
| Crown Pin / existing Gold Pin | Brass/gold could look identical small | Three-point crown and V collar versus smooth coin-medallion body; +40/0 last-fallen display versus after-bank currency |
| Gold Ball / House Ball / Hammer Ball | Metallic recolor would be insufficient | Thin polygonal vault shell, coin slot and triad pips versus marbled resin or heavy crossed reinforcement bands |
| Gold Ball / Gold Pin / Gold Rush | Shared economy symbols obscure reward type | Groups-of-three capped per-roll income versus individual pin payout versus counted Mult; separate spherical/pin/charm identities |
| Splitter Ball / Ghost Ball | A broken shell could imply phasing | Solid joined halves with a central seam and two physical trajectories versus negative-space shell becoming one solid ball |
| Nudge / Reserve Call | Generic swap arrows could look identical | One adjacent rack-map arrow versus two exchange trays and an explicit temporary frame tab |
| Rule Waiver / Encore / Standing Order | All could become generic paper ephemera | Folded exemption permit with diagonal seal versus paired tickets or rigid clipboard; rule badge and frame expiry carry the waiver meaning |

E19 must present a grayscale/silhouette contact sheet and the actual-size color
sheet for each flagged pair; revise any pair that fails before claiming artwork
complete. These written constructions provide a route to distinction, but no
rendered distinction is claimed yet. Do not solve a failed pair with a new color.
