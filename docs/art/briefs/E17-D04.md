## Shared badge and host-overlay standard

Design §15: a modification is an attachment, never a replacement base identity.
The collectible modification card shows the object alone; its icon becomes the
small badge on the host card. Plan a 32px badge and 64px inspection icon, plus a
192px card proof. Use readable geometry, broad negative spaces and the established
warm material palette. Category/rarity symbols and names remain native UI. The
modification's rarity treatment belongs to its own card; it must not change the
host's rarity or hide the host name, stats, charges, illustration or controls.

In play, keep the host's silhouette, material and identifying mark readable.
Use small cosmetic decals/mesh accents with no bodies, collision, mass, trajectory,
score or RNG writes. Only one modification attaches to a host. Check legal hosts
in a mixed rack/hand; shape and placement must distinguish the attachment without
relying on hue, emissive glow or a fully recoloured body. Visual priorities keep
base identity first, attachment second, current activation third.

Every definition supplies exactly six IDs below; none supplies an `in_play` ID.
Do not invent a seventh slot or silently repurpose the base host's asset. The
badge can use the declared icon; construct the described host overlay through the
presentation adapter under the AV claim. If that adapter needs a new persistent
asset contract, coordinate that explicit contract before authoring it. These
briefs do not change a schema, assets, paths or completeness flags.

Resolver events/facts own payouts, conditions, counts and first/last positions.
Physical cues read confirmed contacts/launches; they never predict a score or
animate an unconfirmed success. All display text uses native labels. Original
procedural or recorded-by-author material foley may be commissioned in AV work;
log actual source and hashes then. No existing sound is claimed delivered here.
Reduced motion/flash keeps a static causal mark and exact native outcome; effects
off retains the base identity and essential text. E26 owns physical-device checks.

## Chalk Grip — `ball_mod.chalk_grip`

State: landed in E18-C04; AV-C04 follows in Wave 6. Rarity: common. Tags: `spare`, `mult`.

Authored downside: Nothing on strikes, so it competes with strike builds for the modification slot.

Compatibility exclusions: none authored.

Authored trigger: +2 Mult for each roll thrown with the chalked ball in a frame that ends as a spare (both rolls count when both use it). A strike or an open frame gets nothing.

Required presentation: A badge on the host card and a small overlay on the host in play; the host's own silhouette and marking stay readable. A chalk-dust puff on release in a spare frame.

Badge/card construction: A squared chalk block with a clipped corner, two blunt finger grooves and a short broken dust line. Matte ivory against charcoal; its rectangular silhouette must survive monochrome.

Host overlay: A small broken chalk crescent near the finger holes, leaving the host emblem and most of the body uncovered. Never whiten the whole ball or replace its surface.

Activation and sound brief: A small release dust mark may identify the equipped material, but it must not promise a spare before the frame resolves. Award feedback waits for the actual spare-only per-roll events and shows +2 each. A strike/open has no bonus dust burst. Use one dry chalk scrape per eligible event; retrigger policy follows resolver events.

Reduced motion/flash: Freeze a chalk fleck cluster and show the actual +2/count; no drifting particles.

Recognition check: Distinct from Wood chips and Sweet Spot: block/finger grooves, not debris splinters or concentric rings.

## Echo Charge — `ball_mod.echo_charge`

Authored downside: Cannot charge an Echo Ball, and a glancing first contact wastes it.

Compatibility exclusions: `ball.echo`.

Authored trigger: Retriggers the scoring effects of the first pin the charged ball knocks down directly that roll, once, in that pin's rack slot: Echo Ball's rule on any other ball.

Required presentation: A badge on the host card and a small overlay on the host in play; the host's own silhouette and marking stay readable. Concentric pulses like Echo's, but as a ring on the host ball so the two never read alike.

Badge/card construction: An open annular clip with two nested rings and a clear clasp gap. The gap and two small fastener feet distinguish it from a complete Echo orb.

Host overlay: A thin ring clipped around a small portion of the host, leaving the host’s center emblem visible. Never substitute Echo Ball’s shell or erase House/Hook/Hammer markings.

Activation and sound brief: One pair of concentric pulses begins at the attached ring, then identifies the actual first-direct pin when its scoring effects retrigger. Keep the host visible through the pulse. Audio echoes once at the real repeat; do not imply repeated income or an extra ball. Incompatible with Echo Ball.

Reduced motion/flash: Open ring and one static repeated-pin link; same slot/position and repeat count, no expanding pulse.

Recognition check: REQUIRED FLAG: Echo Charge / Echo Ball. Secondary open clasp ring versus Echo’s complete resonant ball. Test on multiple compatible hosts, and show the incompatible Echo attachment as disallowed by existing UI.

## Bumper Guard — `ball_mod.bumper_guard`

State: authored; waits for its primitive and later AV claim. Rarity: rare. Tags: `safety`, `consistency`, `physics`.

Authored downside: A rescued roll costs 2 Mult (never below 1), and it does nothing for a roll that stays on the lane.

Compatibility exclusions: none authored.

Authored trigger: The first time in a frame a roll thrown with this ball reaches a gutter, it rebounds once off a bumper toward the pins instead of being voided. Pins it then knocks down count; that roll gives −2 Mult, never taking Mult below 1.

Required presentation: A badge on the host card and a small overlay on the host in play; the host's own silhouette and marking stay readable. A visible bumper rail appears only for the rebound; the −2 Mult is named in the explanation.

Badge/card construction: A U-shaped padded rail segment with two square end bolts and a thick inner cushion. The open U is readable at badge size, unlike the round Echo clasp.

Host overlay: Two tiny bumper tabs on the host’s lower-side attachment point; no encircling cage that replaces the ball silhouette.

Activation and sound brief: The rail appears only at the confirmed first gutter rebound of the frame, with a single compression and return. A short padded thump, not an explosive impact.

Reduced motion/flash: Static rail/contact bracket for the actual rebound; spent indicator and −2 remain legible without elastic motion.

Recognition check: Distinct from Rubber Pin and Echo Charge: open padded U with end bolts, no ribbed pin body or concentric pulse. Requires the gutter-rebound primitive before native verification.

## Native acceptance for later AV claims

For each landed modification, buy its real offer, attach it through the existing
choice sheet, inspect the host card badge, owned inventory/rack and binder, then
throw the modified host. Compare bare/modified legal hosts at native size, in
portrait/landscape, monochrome/silhouette, reduced motion/flash and effects off.
Show one eligible and one quiet outcome; include repeat/after-bank boundaries for
Echo Charge, Coin Slot and Gilt Leaf, first/last outcomes for Spearhead, and actual
standing/fallen counts for Lantern/Keystone. Host physics, score, RNG and save data
must match independent resolution; the overlay only observes.

A16/C03's real-body attachment checks provide the cosmetic-body precedent, but
modification badges still need their own host-binding checks. Compare each of the
five flagged pairs together. Confirm the host can still be identified when the
badge or overlay is hidden, and the modification can be identified when the host
material changes. Never claim these native checks from a contact sheet alone.

sounds or modification completeness flags are delivered by this document.
