## Targets and the strategy simulation
`tools/slice_strategies.gd` now takes `--content=`; with `neon` it plays the eight Divisions with the Neon Kit, the specialist weights, two Neon builds (curve: Glow Ball, Prism Pin, Afterimage, Neon Sign; straight: Laser Ball, Laser Level, Ruby and Tempo Pins) and three shared ones. Oil Slick halves what a curve adds, Narrow Lane costs first-roll strikes and Strobe grades a release one step worse (a synthetic model, as the slice's is). The slice's output is byte-identical to before.

200 seeded attempts each, fresh Career:
| Build | Clears Division 1 | Clears Division 2 | Clears Division 3 | Mean matches won (of 24) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neon curve | 62% | 21% | 2% | 3.7 |
| Neon straight | 51% | 10% | 1% | 2.9 |
| Strike chain (House build) | 63% | 9% | 1% | 3.2 |
| Random buyer | 57% | 14% | 2% | 3.3 |
| House kit only | 24% | 0% | 0% | 1.8 |

The same model on the House League (not run before) gives the same shape: its best builds clear Division 2 in 15–16% of attempts and Division 3 in 1%. So Neon's targets are no harder than House's, the curve build is Neon's strongest and straight play stays viable through Division 1, as the spec intends. The finding for E24 is about both Leagues: a single-purpose build of an ordinary player stalls at Division 2–3, and nobody in the model wins a title. The targets stay provisional; nothing was changed here.
